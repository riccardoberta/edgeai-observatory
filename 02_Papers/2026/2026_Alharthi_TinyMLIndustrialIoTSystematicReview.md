# TinyML in Industrial IoT: A Systematic Review of Applications, System Components, and Methodologies

**Full citation:** Alharthi, S., Rashid, M., Aljabri, M. (2026). TinyML in Industrial IoT: A Systematic Review of Applications, System Components, and Methodologies. Sensors, 26(8), 2550. DOI: 10.3390/s26082550

**PDF:** [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/8/2550)

**Linked concepts:** [[Industrial_IoT]]

## Abstract summary

A systematic literature review of 35 peer-reviewed studies (2018-2026) synthesizing TinyML-for-Industrial-IoT work across three dimensions: applications, system components, and methodologies, finding predictive maintenance, equipment monitoring, anomaly detection, and energy management as the dominant use cases, with vibration sensing the most common sensor type.

## Research problem

TinyML-in-Industrial-IoT research had grown across many independent studies with no unified, systematic understanding of the field's application landscape, system-component choices, or methodological practices.

## Key idea

Systematically review and categorize the TinyML-for-IIoT literature along application domain, system architecture, and methodology, rather than surveying by algorithm family alone.

## Technical contribution

A structured taxonomy of TinyML-IIoT applications, system components (sensor types, hardware tiers, runtimes), and methodological practices, synthesized from 35 studies.

## Experimental methodology

Systematic literature review following standard SLR methodology (search, screening, synthesis) across 2018-2026 publications.

## Results

Identifies predictive maintenance, equipment monitoring, anomaly detection, and energy management as the dominant application clusters, and vibration sensing as the most common sensor modality; surfaces gaps in current practice.

## Comparison with the state of the art

The first dedicated systematic review for this concept's specific intersection (TinyML + Industrial IoT), complementing the more general MIMII-dataset and Tiny-MLOps anchors already tracked with a structured, literature-wide view.

## Strengths

Recent (2026), systematically conducted, open-access; directly scoped to this concept rather than TinyML or IIoT generally.

## Weaknesses

Review only, no new empirical results; limited to 35 studies meeting inclusion criteria, so may undercount grey literature/industry reports.

## Limitations

Synthesis-level findings; does not benchmark the surveyed systems against each other on common hardware/datasets.

## Open questions

Which of the surveyed system-component choices (hardware tier, runtime, sensor modality) most strongly predicts deployment success, and is there a standard benchmark comparable to MLPerf Tiny specifically for industrial TinyML tasks?

## Possible extensions

A follow-up quantitative meta-analysis benchmarking a representative subset of the 35 surveyed systems on common hardware.

## Relevance to our research

A recent, dedicated survey-level anchor for this concept, useful as an entry point and gap-analysis reference for future Industrial_IoT work.

## Possible thesis topics

Building a standardized cross-study benchmark for TinyML industrial anomaly/fault detection, addressing the comparability gap this review implicitly identifies.
