# Monthly Report — August 2026

## Emerging trends

**1. The MoE/edge-LLM-serving cluster has grown a fifth leg: standardized measurement infrastructure, not new algorithms.** The [[MoE_Edge_LLM_Serving]] concept is grounded in seven papers (FreeToken, APEX, EdgeXpert, UnionSparse, PolyQ, HeteroMosaic, "Is Your NPU Ready for LLMs?"). Two further August papers add a different kind of contribution to the same cluster: Hydra (IEEE IISWC 2026) is an open-source, 107K-record measurement framework characterizing LLM inference across three Jetson AGX generations, backends, and quantization levels, and "What Actually Runs" does the analogous job for Apple's Neural Engine with a triple-measurement methodology. Both produce genuinely non-obvious empirical findings — Hydra shows quantization reduces memory traffic and energy but does *not* predict power draw monotonically; the Apple Neural Engine (ANE) study shows that accelerator placement is gated by how a computation is *expressed*, not what it computes (a fused RMSNorm is ANE-eligible, its arithmetically identical decomposition is CPU-only). This is the same "diagnose before you build" progression noted in July for on-device LLMs (diagnose → schedule/quantize), now maturing one level further into diagnose-as-open-infrastructure. AirMoE extends the cluster again, moving the MoE-serving bottleneck to the physical/wireless layer (over-the-air aggregation of expert outputs) — a genuinely new angle, though simulation-only so far.

**2. Real-silicon validation is now the norm, not the exception, for edge-AI hardware papers.** Across August's scored weekly picks, at least six report measurements from fabricated silicon rather than simulation or FPGA emulation alone: EdgeXpert (Samsung 28nm), ETHEREAL (custom ASIC, first EV-GNN processor), the EEG auditory-attention-decoding ASIC (GF22FDX 22nm), Lonic (compared against real Apple M4/Nvidia V100 hardware), YAVIN (PIM-capable DRAM prototype), and the Apple ANE study (direct memory-controller byte-counter reads on shipping hardware). This continues a pattern first visible in July (the RISC-V float16 training paper's ISA area-overhead numbers), with increasing frequency — a sign the field is holding itself to a higher reproducibility bar than a year ago.

**3. Architecture/CAD venues, not ML venues, are where this literature is landing.** None of August's highest-relevance picks report a NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR/MLSys acceptance. Instead, the acceptances trace to MICRO 2026 (EdgeXpert), ICCAD 2026 (Lonic), ESWEEK/CODES 2026 with IEEE TCAD (APEX, UnionSparse), ASAP 2026 (FQTree), BioCAS 2026 (the EEG ASIC), and IEEE IISWC 2026 (Hydra) — a genuine sources-coverage consideration for future monitoring, since several of the month's highest-relevance items (APEX, EdgeXpert, Lonic, ETHEREAL, Hydra) surface primarily through arXiv cross-posting rather than direct conference-proceedings monitoring.

## Where things stand on open questions

**Is "spend compute only where it matters" still generating new papers, or has it matured into background practice?** The evidence leans toward the latter: unlike July's four-paper cluster (UnIT, BitFair, AMC, HeteroMosaic), no August pick proposes a new dynamic/input-adaptive compute-allocation *algorithm* at the MAC/bit/token granularity. The closest analogue is architectural rather than algorithmic — ETHEREAL's event-driven sparse processing and RaStream's fixed-memory streaming design both "spend resources only where events occur," but as a hardware/systems design discipline rather than a learned policy. A reasonable reading: the idea has diffused into standard practice for new hardware designs rather than continuing as its own algorithmic research thread.

**Are mainstream ML-systems groups still moving into edge?** David Kaeli's systems-architecture group at Northeastern (with Zlatan Feric, Yanzhi Wang, Amir Taherin) published two papers this month on edge-LLM deployment measurement — the adaptive-RAG-compression study and Hydra — a sustained research thread rather than a one-off, distinct from the FreeToken authors' (Song Han, Ion Stoica, Matei Zaharia, Kurt Keutzer) larger-profile excursion into edge serving. The pattern (a systems lab making edge-LLM characterization its focus across consecutive papers) suggests edge-LLM measurement is becoming a defined sub-specialty with its own repeat players.

## New research directions

First, **a standardized, open measurement methodology for MCU/NPU-class inference, not just Jetson/Apple-silicon-class.** Hydra and the ANE study both build rigorous, open, reproducible characterization infrastructure — but both target relatively powerful edge accelerators (Jetson AGX, Apple Neural Engine). Neither has an analogue at the [[Cortex-M]] / microcontroller-NPU tier. Porting Hydra's phase-aware timing-and-telemetry schema, or the ANE study's "does operator expression gate accelerator placement" question, to Arm Ethos-class or RISC-V-[[NPU]] targets is a well-scoped, high-value, currently unclaimed research gap.

Second, **a rigorous definition of the "edge-native" boundary.** This is a recurring tension across the MoE/edge-LLM-serving literature (PolyQ/HeteroMosaic, the APEX/EdgeXpert/UnionSparse cluster, FreeToken). Nobody has yet proposed a quantitative criterion (memory budget, power envelope, cost tier) that would let the field say where "edge-native MoE serving" stops and "genuine TinyML" begins. A short position-paper-style contribution formalizing this boundary — perhaps anchored to MLPerf Tiny's device classes versus MLPerf Inference's edge category — would be a citable, low-cost, high-visibility contribution rather than a response to someone else's paper.

At the standards/ecosystem layer, the MLCommons Tiny working group continues to be the most structurally influential actor, though no new benchmark round was released this month.

## Software releases

Apache TVM v0.26.0 shipped 11 August 2026, adding — among other changes — Z3-backed analyzer proving, a FlashInfer 0.6.3 KVCache backend, and, notably for [[RISC-V]], new RISC-V target device keys. This is the first concrete TVM signal in two months that touches RISC-V directly rather than GPU/WASM-class backends, worth weighing against the June/July "toolchain bifurcation" observation (TVM's center of gravity drifting toward GPU/accelerator-class hardware, away from MCU-native use) — one release isn't enough to reverse that reading, but it is a data point in the other direction. ONNX Runtime v1.29.0 shipped 17 August 2026 with CPython 3.14 wheel support across Linux/Windows/macOS; no edge/microcontroller-specific changes were confirmed in this release. CMSIS-NN and TensorFlow Lite Micro (now rebranded LiteRT for Microcontrollers) show no new confirmed tagged release this month.

The month's most consequential ecosystem software event is not a version bump: Qualcomm's acquisition of Modular closed 29 July 2026 (announced 24 June, ~$4B all-stock). Modular — founded by Chris Lattner, the creator of LLVM, Swift, and [[MLIR]] — built the Mojo language and MAX inference platform for running AI models across CPUs, GPUs, NPUs, and custom accelerators without per-target rewrites. Qualcomm states Mojo, MAX, and Modular Cloud continue as products with expanded investment across CPUs, GPUs, NPUs, and custom silicon. This directly strengthens the consolidation thesis around NPU vendors converging on MLIR as shared compiler infrastructure ([[2026_Absar_HexagonMLIR]], the Qualcomm Hexagon case) — Qualcomm now owns both a major MLIR-adjacent compiler stack and its own NPU silicon roadmap, worth tracking as a potential inflection point for [[MLIR]]'s role in edge deployment specifically (versus its current center of gravity in datacenter/GPU-class AI infrastructure).

## Research and thesis opportunities

A standardized measurement framework for MCU/NPU-class edge inference (Master's/PhD; [[Cortex-M]] x [[NPU]]): extend Hydra's phase-aware timing/telemetry schema or the Apple ANE study's "expression gates placement" methodology to Arm Ethos-U/RISC-V-NPU-class hardware, where no comparable open, reproducible benchmark harness currently exists.

Quantifying the "edge-native" boundary (Master's-scale position/measurement study; bridges [[MoE_Edge_LLM_Serving]]): propose and validate a quantitative criterion — memory budget, power envelope, or cost tier — separating "edge-native" serving (FreeToken, HeteroMosaic-class: laptop/workstation GPUs) from genuine TinyML deployment ([[Cortex-M]]/[[RISC-V]]-class), using MLPerf Tiny vs. MLPerf Inference's own device-class definitions as a starting anchor.

A second anchor for edge AI hardware security (Master's; extends [[Hardware_Security_of_Edge_AI_Accelerators]]): evaluate YAVIN's TEE-into-PIM architecture, or a comparable design, against the quantization spectrum (INT8/INT4/mixed) already tracked under [[Quantization]].

Re-testing the microTVM/RISC-V trajectory (light-touch, ongoing tracking; [[microTVM_TVM]] x [[RISC-V]]): TVM v0.26.0's new RISC-V target device keys are a small but concrete counter-signal to the "toolchain bifurcation away from MCU-class targets" trend noted in June — worth checking whether TVM's RISC-V support targets MCU-class or accelerator-class RISC-V.
