# Monthly Report — September 2026

## Emerging trends

**1. On microcontroller-class NPUs, what decides whether a model runs is the accelerator's operator set and runtime, not the model's size.** September produced an unusually dense set of independent demonstrations of the same fact. A 37k-parameter speech enhancer (LiSenNet, arXiv:2609.29866) and a transformer line-segment detector (NPLSD, arXiv:2609.25022) both had to be re-architected around the STM32N6 Neural-ART's static-int8 graph — recurrent bottlenecks, attention, grid-sampling and normalization have no accelerator primitives, and a single self-attention score tensor (39 MB) exceeds on-chip memory — while an audio-visual speech recognizer (NAVIR, arXiv:2609.24391) was factorised into 2D-convolution-only modules because BrainChip Akida executes nothing else. A CubeSat Earth-observation pipeline on the same STM32N6 (Capogrosso and Magno, ACM/IEEE ICCPS 2026, arXiv:2603.20174) frames its whole deployment method as "hardware-aware operator mapping" that maximizes Neural-ART operator coverage, and a crowd-counting deployment on the same part (Abdalla, OpenReview 2026) introduces an integer-compatible alignment operator for the same reason. The constraint extends beyond inference: QScheduler (Amaral et al., arXiv:2607.18802) resorts to zeroth-order, forward-only gradient estimation precisely because the Neural-ART has no backward-pass primitives, giving the first INT8 on-device training on that NPU. Taken with the finding that vendor NPUs "own" quantization — a bring-your-own QDQ graph fails silently on one NPU and loudly on another (arXiv:2609.16085) — and that excluding layers from quantization "to be safe" can fragment the graph and disable operator fusion on ARM64 CPUs (RAMP, arXiv:2609.28262), the picture is consistent across at least five unrelated groups and three accelerator families (Neural-ART, Akida, and the Hexagon/DX-M1 vendor NPUs of the portability study): deployability at the [[NPU]] / [[Cortex-M]] tier is a toolchain and runtime property that neither MAC counts nor bare-metal benchmark numbers capture. The [[NPU]] concept, which treats NPUs primarily as throughput accelerators, and the Frameworks branch, which has no page for vendor NPU toolchains, both lack this dimension.

**2. "Validate on target, or predict before you deploy": quantization outcomes are no longer treated as predictable from bit width.** Six papers from five unrelated groups in two weeks converge on the finding that the same nominal quantization gives different outcomes depending on architecture, kernel and target, and then split into two responses. The measurement response holds the artifact fixed and measures: INT8 predictions are bit-identical across targets only when two targets share an integer kernel (1000/1000) and otherwise agree 958–965/1000 even though top-1 accuracy is preserved, and the sign of the INT8 speed-up is set by whether the CPU has a dot-product ISA (arXiv:2609.16085); the same bit width costs 2.8 UTMOS on one text-to-speech model and 0.07 on another, and real int8 kernels can be slower than 4-bit weight kernels on the same machine (arXiv:2609.28974); gradient- and weight-based layer-sensitivity proxies fail catastrophically on 4 and 2 of 8 model–hardware configurations respectively, while Jensen–Shannon divergence between full-precision and quantized layer outputs never does (RAMP). The prediction response builds cheap offline proxies with explicit accept/reject/defer decisions: PreDE calibrates two thresholds on offline action deviations and issued 21 of 28 configuration decisions before any closed-loop robot trial, all matching the observed labels (arXiv:2609.19441); "Quantization Price" prices every layer configuration's output-error covariance by downstream curvature, ½ Tr(H Σ), so formats, granularities and codebooks become comparable candidates in one table (arXiv:2609.28270); VLAQuantBench shows across 94,574 simulation episodes that protecting a few specific layers, not the bit width, decides whether a quantized vision-language-action model still works (arXiv:2609.25376). The measurement thread extends the known gap on edge-inference measurement infrastructure down toward the MCU tier; the prediction thread is a distinct algorithmic direction inside [[Quantization]] that no existing concept page describes.

**3. In-memory computing is now the densest hardware thread, and it is being attacked from three independent angles.** Within one month: a fault-tolerant MTJ-based stochastic in-memory architecture validated at 14nm ([[2026_Razi_FALCON]]); a certified selective-inference framework that computes an exact per-chip error bound for heterogeneous analog in-memory accelerators and runs only the single best chip for a given energy budget (RACE-AIMC, arXiv:2609.03149); a fabricated 28nm digital in-memory-computing AI microcontroller that makes on-chip fine-tuning as efficient as inference and reports MLPerf Tiny results, 1.34 TOPS/W and up to 22× better energy-delay product than prior digital-IMC MCUs (EPIC, IEEE TCAS-I, DOI 10.1109/TCSI.2026.3725278); a fabricated 40nm event-based digital compute-in-memory accelerator for spiking networks with bitwise-reconfigurable operand resolution and layer-wise hybrid stationarity (FlexSpIM, arXiv:2609.08446); an experimental study showing temperature-induced degradation in analog DNN inference is driven mainly by systematic non-idealities rather than stochastic noise, so hardware-in-the-loop training and temperature-aware calibration retain accuracy best (arXiv:2609.15527); and, from a group unrelated to any of the above, a fabricated 65nm memristor-based in-memory-computing system-on-chip with a zig-zag selection-line topology for depthwise convolution, measured at 21.3 TOPS/W and reaching 80.36% on Visual Wake Words with a tailored MobileNetV1 — on par with the 4-bit software model (Song et al., Advanced Intelligent Systems 2026, DOI 10.1002/aisy.202501225). Three distinct strategies for the same underlying problem — imperfect physical compute — are now visible: tolerate it architecturally (FALCON), calibrate or train it away (the temperature study), or certify around it statistically (RACE-AIMC). The emerging-NVM in-memory computing gap in the Hardware taxonomy now has the second, independently-authored, measured-silicon anchor it was waiting for.

**4. The MCU tier is where real-silicon validation is strongest, and the data is increasingly MLPerf-Tiny-anchored.** Fabricated-chip measurements appeared for EPIC (28nm, MLPerf Tiny), FlexSpIM (40nm, IBM DVS gesture), the memristor SoC (65nm, Visual Wake Words), the SNN non-uniform memory-partitioning design (28nm synthesis, arXiv:2608.30444), plus deployed-board measurements with a power profiler for FORGE on ESP32-S3 (8.3 mJ per adaptation step, arXiv:2609.01683), SAMSTeG on ESP32 (27.6 mJ per inference, IEEE ESL, DOI 10.1109/LES.2026.3732944), the STM32 EEG dementia benchmark (45 KB SRAM, BMC Neurology, DOI 10.1186/s12883-026-05315-4) and the smart-glasses study on an nRF52840 (EWSN 2026 EMERGE). Two of these use MLPerf Tiny workloads as the common yardstick (EPIC; the glasses paper's VWW variant), and the memristor SoC reports VWW accuracy against MLPerf Tiny's 80% quality target. This continues the real-silicon trend already visible in the preceding two months, but with the center of gravity moving from accelerator-class chips to MCU-class parts.

## Where things stand on open questions

**Is there an open, reusable measurement methodology at the MCU/NPU tier yet?** Not quite, but the gap narrowed on both sides. From above, the cross-platform INT8 study (arXiv:2609.16085) is the first reusable, artifact-fixed, open methodology (scripts plus 32 measurement reports) spanning ARM/x86 CPUs, Jetson Orin iGPU and NVDLA, and two vendor NPUs — it stops above Cortex-M and Ethos-U. From below, EPIC supplies MLPerf-Tiny-grounded fine-tuning numbers for a single chip, the glasses paper quantifies how far bare-metal MLPerf Tiny numbers are from a real wearable (effective budget ~270 kB flash / ~200 kB SRAM after firmware; inference only 37% of a pipeline iteration), and a Scholar-surfaced design-space exploration of TinyML workloads on heterogeneous processing targets for wearables (Penner et al., IEEE-EMBS 2026, OpenReview) addresses the same realism question. An Elsevier *Array* systematic review (Mahfouz et al., 2026) reportedly quantifies up to 60% error between digital-twin energy/latency estimates and real microcontroller measurements; that figure is recorded at snippet level only and should be verified before citation. The "measurement" and "operator-set" findings are now clearly two faces of one question — what a benchmark harness at this tier would need to capture is which operators fall back to the CPU and what that costs, not just MACs per second — and the two known gaps should be reviewed together.

**Is "edge-native" LLM serving converging on a definition?** No, but the industry is standardizing the workload rather than the tier. MLPerf Inference v6.1 (16 September) added an Agentic Edge Inference benchmark alongside its five existing edge tests, defining "edge" as multi-turn agentic workloads on hardware such as the AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395 — laptop-class. Qualcomm's Snapdragon Summit (22–23 September) claimed 30B-parameter mixture-of-experts models running from flash on the Hexagon NPU, 35% faster and 33% better performance-per-watt than the prior generation, and announced that its near-memory "High-Bandwidth Compute" architecture will come from the data-center line to Snapdragon. On the research side, mzCache (MobiCom 2026, arXiv:2609.01338) addresses OS-level memory pressure on on-device LLMs under multitasking (2.1–5.5× faster time-to-first-token on Android), TierKV predicts KV-cache demand from prefill hidden states to tier tokens across exact, low-rank and flash-offloaded storage on three mobile SoCs (arXiv:2609.21172), and a block-diffusion LLM accelerator co-designs wide-I/O LPDDR, mixed-precision KV compression and FFN weight handling for batch-one Jetson-class inference (arXiv:2609.01084). Every one of these sits at the smartphone or Jetson tier; the boundary question raised in [[MoE_Edge_LLM_Serving]] and [[Generative_EdgeAI]] stands.

**Has "spend compute only where it matters" fully diffused into practice?** Largely yes, with a twist. The clearest new instance is a scheduler, not a model: an empirically calibrated DVFS controller with time-domain guards and derivative triggers eliminates thermal throttling on a passively-cooled Raspberry Pi 5 running YOLOv8n for 30 minutes, with 6.8% higher frame rate and 1.9% less energy per frame than a reactive baseline (arXiv:2609.04705). Albireo gates detector invocations with a Kalman filter on Jetson AGX Thor and Orin, cutting energy 12.1–17.6% within ±1.2 AP points (ACM/IEEE SEC 2026, arXiv:2609.29648). The twist is the finding, from the compute-matched early-exit study for on-device speech enhancement (arXiv:2609.29867), that on static-graph NPUs a depth-varying network must be split into several graphs orchestrated by a policy — dynamic compute allocation is itself subject to the operator-set constraint of trend 1.

## New research directions

First, **on-device training on microcontrollers is shifting from feasibility to resource accounting and to NPU-constrained formulations.** Papers appearing across the summer and September, from six unrelated groups, treat the cost of training as the object of study rather than its possibility: an energy-aware on-device training study measures actual STM32 energy across back-propagation depths to test whether MACs are a valid proxy (eODL, EWSN 2026 WiP); an SRAM-budgeting framework accounts for peak training memory and selects feasible trainable suffixes with a configuration-dependent crossover analysis (Jeong and Oh, Journal of Systems Architecture 2026); TinyMQT bounds the peak SRAM footprint of back-propagation through mixed-quantization training (IEEE Embedded Systems Letters 2026); Sparse-BP integrates stochastic sparse back-propagation for MCU boards (IEEE 2026); EPIC co-designs silicon so that fine-tuning costs no more than inference; QScheduler and FORGE both drop the backward pass entirely — zeroth-order gradient sampling on an INT8 NPU, and forward-only re-normalization of folded convolutions to restore test-time adaptation on BN-folded integer models (+20.9 points versus +24.9 for gradient-based TENT, adapting only 3 of 21 layers) — because the deployed integer runtime offers no gradient primitives. TinyUDE adds a solver-free existence proof for on-MCU fitting of dynamical systems within 61.3 kB (arXiv:2609.26972), and Xronos shows pipeline parallelism is ineffective for collaborative fine-tuning on CPU-only edge devices (5.75× higher compute-stall ratio than GPU devices, arXiv:2609.19909). The [[On-device_Learning]] and [[Continual_Learning]] pages describe the first-order, float-capable regime; the integer-only, NPU-constrained, SRAM- and energy-budgeted regime is a distinct sub-area that now has enough independent anchors to be written down.

Second, **robotics is consolidating as an EdgeAI application domain, but at the GPU-onboard tier.** Four unrelated groups published on-robot deployment work in three weeks — mixed-precision rigid-body-dynamics accelerators (APEX-RBD, arXiv:2609.05161), offline prediction of quantization degradation validated in 450 Franka trials (PreDE), recovery of 63–87% width-pruned VLA backbones by offline hidden-state distillation with on-board measurements on a 6-DoF manipulator (arXiv:2609.19579), and native W4A4 TensorRT execution of GR00T and π0.5 on Jetson AGX Orin with 80-trial real-robot validation per configuration (FoldQuantVLA, arXiv:2609.24433). NVIDIA's positioning of the Jetson Orin Nano 2 (announced 25 August, 78 TOPS, 2× its predecessor, available first half of 2027) and the Thor T3000/T2000 modules (Q1 2027) explicitly as "robotics computers" says where the industry expects this work to run. The open question for the taxonomy is whether "Robotics" is one Applications concept spanning the custom-accelerator and Jetson tiers, or whether large-model-on-robot work belongs closer to [[Generative_EdgeAI]].

Third, **Jetson-class hardware keeps appearing as a deployment target without a characterization literature of its own.** Albireo (Thor, Orin), FoldQuantVLA (Orin), PreDE and the pruned-VLA paper (onboard GPU), the block-diffusion accelerator (modeled Jetson-class) and TierKV (mobile SoCs) all measure on this tier, yet the only microarchitecture-level characterization remains [[2025_Chakraborty_ProfilingJetsonVisionInference]] plus the phase-aware framework of [[2026_Taherin_Hydra]]. With a new Orin generation and a mid-range Thor line arriving in early 2027, a second independent characterization paper is likely, and the Hardware branch's lack of an edge-GPU node will become more visible.

## Influential research groups

Charlotte Frenkel's group at TU Delft is the clearest "watch" case for the second consecutive month: after the ETHEREAL event-graph processor ([[2026_Kneip_ETHEREAL]], with Marian Verhelst and Davide Scaramuzza), FlexSpIM is a second fabricated event-driven chip from the same group within weeks, and together they define what the [[Event-Driven_Neuromorphic_Accelerators]] concept should mean in 2026 — measured silicon, standard event-camera benchmarks, and flexibility (per-layer dataflow, operand resolution) as the new axis beyond raw efficiency. David Kaeli's systems-architecture group at Northeastern (Amir Taherin, Yanzhi Wang, with José Cano and Bin Ren) followed Hydra with Albireo, confirming that Jetson-tier energy measurement is a sustained program rather than a one-off. ETH Zürich is the most productive single source of MCU-tier deployment work: Michele Magno's group with the CubeSat STM32N6 pipeline (Capogrosso and Magno), and Cristian Cioflan, Lukas Cavigelli, Manuele Rusci and co-authors with on-device domain learning for keyword spotting within 357 mJ on an ultra-low-power MCU (IEEE Internet of Things Journal 2026). Yangming Shi's group (PreDE, VLAQuantBench) is defining the evaluation methodology for quantized robot policies. Mingoo Seok's group at Columbia (EPIC) is the reference point for MCU-class in-memory computing with on-chip fine-tuning. M. Hassan Najafi appears on both FALCON (in-memory stochastic computing) and NPLSD (operator-level NPU characterization), an unusual bridge between the two hardware threads above. Clément Laroche and co-authors (LiSenNet redesign; early-exit speech enhancement with Riccardo Miccini) produced the two most carefully measured STM32N6 audio papers of the month. At the standards layer, MLCommons remains the structurally most influential actor through MLPerf Inference v6.1's definition of edge agentic workloads.

## Software releases

ONNX Runtime 1.30.0 shipped on 10 September 2026 with several changes that matter below the data-center tier: fused LinearAttention kernels for Arm64 NEON and SVE, Arm SVE i8mm INT8 QGEMM kernels, a KleidiAI Q4 prepacking fix and INT4 weight-prepacking optimizations on CPU, INT4 paged KV caches with per-channel scales, restored runtime vector-extension checks on [[RISC-V]], and CPU FP16 Gemm/MatMul now gated on hardware acceleration with FP32 fallback. For [[ONNX_Runtime]] on [[Cortex-A]]-class devices this is the most substantive release since 1.29; it also sharpens the vendor-NPU point of trend 1 — the ArmNN execution provider was removed in an earlier 2026 release in favor of the MLAS/KleidiAI CPU path or the QNN provider for Qualcomm hardware, so the choice of integer kernel is increasingly made by the runtime, not the user.

Apache TVM v0.27.0 passed its release vote on 25 September 2026 (8 binding and non-binding +1 votes, no objections). The release brings LLVM 23 compatibility, shared-KV attention with configurable sliding windows and KV-cache checkpoint primitives, TensorRT engine construction during code generation, a table-driven PTX dialect with tcgen05 support, broader ONNX/PyTorch frontend coverage and quantized SSD inference fixes in the TFLite frontend, and WebGPU/WASM improvements. Nothing in the release notes touches microTVM or MCU-class targets; after August's RISC-V target device keys, this release reads as a return to the GPU/accelerator center of gravity described in the taxonomy's field notes on [[microTVM_TVM]].

LLVM 23.1.0 was released on 26 August 2026, with point releases 23.1.1 and 23.1.2 (22 September) following the two-week cadence; [[MLIR]] ships inside it, and the TVM release above is the first downstream edge compiler to declare LLVM 23 compatibility. CMSIS-NN 8.0.0, released on 21 August 2026, deserves a mention it did not receive in August: it adds an experimental, fully opt-in floating-point extension (float16/float32 operator APIs aligned with the quantized ones, optimized convolution, depthwise-convolution and pooling kernels, enabled by `ARM_NN_ENABLE_F16`/`ARM_NN_ENABLE_F32`), Python bindings for buffer-size functions, a Thumb-2 requantize optimization, and fixes to int8/int16 LSTM state persistence. Float kernels in [[CMSIS-NN]] are directly relevant to the on-device-training direction above, where the gradient computation is the part that resists integer-only execution. In the TensorFlow Lite Micro repository, three pull requests merged between 28 and 30 September restructure the project toward a standalone codebase — migrating TFLM-owned components under `tensorflow/lite/micro/`, decoupling the C API from LiteRT, then restoring LiteRT C-compatibility declarations — a governance signal that [[TensorFlow_Lite_Micro]] is being separated from the LiteRT core rather than absorbed into it. No new tagged TFLM release was confirmed. For [[Edge_Impulse]] the only September item found is Ambiq's reported announcement (8 September) of Edge Impulse ecosystem support for its Apollo4 Plus MCU; no platform release notes were located.

## Benchmarks

MLPerf Inference v6.1 results were published on 16 September 2026 with a record 30 submitting organizations, 10 datacenter and 6 edge benchmarks, two of them new (End-to-End RAG, and Agentic Edge Inference for multi-turn workloads such as agentic coding), speculative decoding supported in the interactive scenario, and five newly benchmarked processors including the AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395 and Intel Arc Pro B70. For this Observatory the Agentic Edge Inference test is the notable item: it is the first standardized definition of an "edge" LLM workload from MLCommons, and it anchors "edge" at the laptop/workstation tier — a concrete reference point for the boundary question above, and the natural counterpart to [[MLPerf_Tiny]] in any attempt to formalize where edge-native serving ends. MLPerf Tiny itself had no new round; v1.4 (July 2026) remains current. The month's MCU-tier benchmark signal came instead from papers: EPIC's MLPerf Tiny fine-tuning numbers, the smart-glasses paper's observation that the MLPerf Tiny VWW INT8 reference (325.5 kB) does not fit a real wearable while a 186 kB α=0.18 variant exceeds the 80% quality target, and the memristor SoC's VWW result — three independent uses of MLPerf Tiny workloads as a common yardstick outside the official submission process.

## Datasets

No new dataset extending or replacing Speech Commands or Visual Wake Words was confirmed. Two developments bear on [[TinyML_Reference_Datasets]] nonetheless. For [[Keyword_Spotting]], SAMSTeG's evaluation protocol — four noise types at SNRs from 20 dB to 0 dB, reporting that state-of-the-art small models lose more than 25 points on average at 0 dB — functions as a de-facto robustness extension of Speech Commands, while CircleMatch (arXiv:2609.20070) evaluates 1k–7k-parameter models on Speech Commands v1/v2 and the Multilingual Spoken Words Corpus micro subsets; the comprehensive review of small-footprint keyword spotting by Garai and Samui (Neurocomputing vol. 695, September 2026) organizes the field into seven technique families and is the citable survey for this concept. For [[Human_Activity_Recognition]], a PRISMA-style review of TinyML for HAR (Lamaakal, Yahyati, Maleh, El Makkaoui, Ouahbi; MDPI *Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction* 8(9):271, 5 September 2026) covers 47 studies from 2020 to May 2026 and catalogs the benchmark datasets in use, while EdgeHAR (arXiv:2609.14498, ACM DOI 10.1145/3798063.3837321) and two companion preprints on virtual-IMU augmentation (arXiv:2609.16768) and domain generalization (arXiv:2609.14863) confirm that acquisition shift, not model capacity, is the branch's central data problem.

## Research and thesis opportunities

An operator-set compatibility audit for microcontroller NPUs (Master's; [[NPU]] × [[MLPerf_Tiny]] × [[TensorFlow_Lite_Micro]]): export the MLPerf Tiny models and a handful of modern small architectures (a sub-band speech enhancer, a line-segment detector, a small transformer) through the vendor toolchains for STM32N6 Neural-ART, Arm Ethos-U85 and BrainChip Akida, and tabulate which operators fall back to the CPU with the measured latency and energy penalty. This would give the operator-set gap a Benchmarks-style anchor and is the most direct route to the MCU/NPU-tier measurement methodology the field still lacks.

Extending the INT8-portability protocol to the MCU tier (Master's; [[Quantization]] × [[CMSIS-NN]] × [[ONNX_Runtime]]): port the artifact-fixed, scales-fixed protocol of arXiv:2609.16085 to TFLM + CMSIS-NN, microTVM and an Ethos-U path, and test whether the "1000/1000 agreement only when the integer kernel is shared" invariance holds where operator-fusion rules differ again; RAMP's 13-metric sensitivity study is a natural companion to repeat on the same targets.

Resource accounting for integer-only on-device training (Master's or PhD; [[On-device_Learning]] × [[Cortex-M]] × [[NPU]]): combine eODL's energy-versus-back-propagation-depth measurements, the SRAM-budgeting framework and QScheduler's zeroth-order NPU training into one cost model that predicts energy, peak SRAM and convergence for a given (model, trainable suffix, gradient method, target) tuple, validated on an STM32N6 and a Cortex-M55 without NPU; CMSIS-NN 8.0's new float kernels make a first-order baseline on the same hardware feasible for the first time.

Certified selective inference beyond analog in-memory computing (PhD-scale; bridges RACE-AIMC, the temperature study and the memristor SoC): the three strategies for imperfect accelerators identified above have not been compared on the same substrate. A study that takes one fabricated or emulated analog/memristive array, applies hardware-in-the-loop calibration, per-chip statistical certification and a fault-tolerant encoding in turn, and reports accuracy, energy and coverage under the same temperature and variation sweeps would be a strong, citable contribution to a Hardware concept that is about to be formalized.

Pre-deployment prediction of quantization degradation outside robotics (Master's; [[Quantization]] × [[Biosignals]] or [[Predictive_Maintenance]]): PreDE's accept/reject/defer rule and the "Quantization Price" curvature pricing are general; a thesis could calibrate either on a quantized biosignal classifier or industrial anomaly detector where the "closed-loop" evaluation is a field trial, and test whether the deferred set catches the catastrophic configurations that VLAQuantBench and the TTS study show are architecture-specific.

Quantifying the "edge-native" boundary (Master's-scale position/measurement study; [[MoE_Edge_LLM_Serving]] × [[Generative_EdgeAI]]): MLPerf Inference v6.1's Agentic Edge Inference test and MLPerf Tiny now bracket the question from both ends. A short paper that runs one small LLM workload across the two benchmark definitions and a Jetson Orin Nano / Snapdragon-class device in between, and proposes a memory-, power- and cost-based criterion for where edge-native serving stops, would fill a gap that none of the serving papers has addressed.
