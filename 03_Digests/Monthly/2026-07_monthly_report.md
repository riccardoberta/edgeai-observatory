# Monthly Report — July 2026

## Emerging trends

Three threads run through the July literature and ecosystem, and — importantly — they are the *same* three threads viewed from different layers of the stack, not three unrelated observations.

**1. "Spend compute only where it matters" is consolidating into a genuine research program.** The single strongest cross-paper signal this month is dynamic, data-dependent allocation of compute. Four independently-authored papers attack it from four different hardware nodes: UnIT does input-adaptive *inference-time* pruning on a commodity MSP430 by turning MAC decisions into threshold checks; BitFair learns per-layer *bit-level* early-termination thresholds in taped-out 12nm silicon; AMC allocates *per-token* rank and bit-width by saliency for transformer inference; and, at the systems layer, HeteroMosaic gates *when* co-executing accelerators pays off via a heterogeneous roofline. These are not incremental variations on one method — they are the same principle ("don't process easy inputs as hard as difficult ones") re-derived at the granularity of MACs, bits, tokens, and whole operators, and it directly extends [[Pruning]], [[Quantization]], and [[Compression]] with a unifying concept they otherwise lack individually. The MLPerf Tiny v1.4 results (below) confirm the same idea has reached production: Syntiant's NDP120 runs the streaming wake-word benchmark at a 3.3% duty cycle — the device does nothing 97% of the time — "spend compute only where it matters" expressed as a system-level power strategy rather than a per-operation trick.

**2. On-device LLM/SLM inference has become a real, distinct sub-field with its own methodology, not just a hype adjacent to TinyML.** Across July it acquired a full research arc: on-device *personalization* of sub-1B SLMs framed as a [[Continual_Learning]] stability problem; a *diagnostic* measurement study of where energy and latency actually go on mobile [[NPU]]s ("Is Your NPU Ready for LLMs?"); and then *constructive* follow-ons — HeteroMosaic's iGPU+NPU co-scheduling and PolyQ's compile-time fractional-bit [[Quantization]] for edge CPUs. The "diagnose → schedule/quantize" progression within a single month is a healthy sign of a maturing area (see [[MoE_Edge_LLM_Serving]]). At the ecosystem layer the same trend is visible in silicon: NVIDIA's Jetson Thor T2000/T3000 (announced 15 July) ship with Cosmos 3 Edge, a 4-billion-parameter world-foundation model built to run on-device, and Arm's Ethos-U85 push (Alif, Infineon) adds native Transformer operators (MATMUL/GATHER/TRANSPOSE) at the milliwatt tier. This remains a heavier weight class than the Cortex-M-class deployments this Observatory mostly tracks, but it converges on the same concerns (quantization, memory footprint, latency, scheduling).

**3. The "compute boundary" is dissolving into a heterogeneous continuum, and on-device *training* is following inference down it.** July's papers show the classic in-sensor-vs-on-MCU boundary is now a spectrum that also includes training: RISC-V single-core float16 *training* on standard ISA extensions; repurposing an inference-only Hailo-8L accelerator to do frozen-backbone feature extraction for on-device *adaptation*; and in-memory-computing (ECRAM) continual learning. A recurring sub-motif — "freeze most of the network, cheaply update a small part" — links three late-July papers and connects back to established [[On-device_Learning]] work (TinyTL, TinyTrain). The important shift is that on-device training moved this month from aspiration to characterized systems: the RISC-V paper quantifies the ISA area overhead of adding `Zfh` float16 (+1.15% LUT6, +0.05% FF), which makes "is on-device training worth the silicon?" an answerable question rather than a slogan.

## New research directions

Two directions are ready for deeper investment because multiple converging papers now triangulate them rather than a lone data point.

First, a **unified theory of dynamic compute allocation**. UnIT, BitFair, AMC, and NVFP4's disentangled ablation each pick one granularity and one hardware node. The open question — genuinely survey-scale and thesis-generating — is whether there is a portable principle that predicts, for a given hardware budget, at which granularity (MAC / bit / token / operator) dynamic allocation pays off, and whether the savings survive on commodity MCUs without the custom silicon that BitFair and AMC assume. This connects [[Pruning]], [[Quantization]], and [[Compression]] and would be a real conceptual contribution rather than another point solution.

Second, **on-device training as a first-class, characterized design problem**. Three July papers freeze most of the network and differ only in *where* the cheap-to-train part runs (low-precision core, offloaded frozen backbone, in-memory computing). A natural research program is to build the missing comparison: for a fixed MCU-class budget, which of float16-on-core / int8-on-core / frozen-backbone-offload wins on the memory–accuracy–energy Pareto front, and can the "which subset to train" decision be predicted rather than hand-tuned? This extends [[On-device_Learning]], [[RISC-V]], and [[Quantization]].

## Influential research groups

Mohsen Imani's group (UC Irvine) appears with PolyQ, an ICCAD-2026-accepted quantization compiler; Deming Chen's group (UIUC) with HeteroMosaic, accepted to MICRO 2026 — both landing at top architecture venues, both on the edge-LLM-efficiency thread. At the ecosystem/standards layer, the most influential actors are arguably not individual labs but the MLCommons Tiny working group (which set the shared energy methodology now used to compare nine vendors head-to-head) and the vendors submitting to it — STMicroelectronics and Syntiant recur here and in the taxonomy's hardware coverage. The RISC-V-training group (Hubinet, Moellic et al., CEA-Leti / Mines Saint-Étienne) is building open, reproducible embedded-training infrastructure directly usable by the lab, making them a collaboration candidate rather than merely a citation.

## Benchmarks

**MLPerf Tiny v1.4 results were released on 28 July 2026.** The round closed with 25 system configurations from nine organizations (Andes, ASYGN, DeepGate, Kai Jiang, Qualcomm, Renesas, STMicroelectronics, Syntiant, University of Leeds), of which five were first-time submitters — more than doubling the participant count from v1.3. Every result is measured against the same task, model, accuracy target, and energy methodology, an antidote to vendor-claim noise. Concrete, citable data points from the round:

STMicroelectronics reports that enabling the hardware signal processor on the STM32U3 reduces image-classification inference time by up to 76.0% and power by 23.3% versus the same Cortex-M33 configuration without it — a clean, reproducible number for [[Cortex-M]] and [[DSP]]. Syntiant's NDP120 runs the streaming wake-word benchmark at a 3.3% duty cycle (see trend #1) — a system-level instance of dynamic compute allocation on a [[Keyword_Spotting]] workload. The University of Leeds reports median throughput of ~1,849.7 inferences/s for image classification on a Versal-based heterogeneous platform with a custom on-fabric batch-norm engine at 313 MHz — a strong [[FPGA]] data point.

The round's own framing — that the field is "maturing beyond CPU-only baselines," with hardware acceleration, streaming workloads, and heterogeneous compute as the defining characteristics — corroborates trends #1 and #3 above from the benchmark side.

## Research and thesis opportunities

A portable framework for dynamic compute allocation (PhD-scale; [[Pruning]] x [[Quantization]] x [[Compression]]): take UnIT (MAC-level), BitFair (bit-level), and AMC (token-level) and ask whether one hardware-budget-aware model predicts which granularity to use, and whether the savings survive on a commodity Cortex-M/RISC-V without custom silicon — three independent papers already triangulate this.

On-device training Pareto study on a single RISC-V core (Master's/PhD; [[RISC-V]] x [[On-device_Learning]] x [[Quantization]]): extend the float16-training paper by comparing `Zfh`/`Zvfh` float16 training against int8/quantized training (cf. Deutel 2024) on the same core, mapping the memory/accuracy/energy front. Open-source infrastructure already exists (AIfES-based), so this is unusually low-friction to start.

Reproduce and extend MLPerf Tiny v1.4 on lab hardware (Master's; [[Cortex-M]] x [[DSP]] x [[Keyword_Spotting]]): replicate ST's STM32U3 "hardware signal processor on/off" measurement (the −76% inference-time result) under the standardized MLPerf Tiny energy methodology, then extend it to a task not in the suite.

FP4 quantization from paper to silicon (Master's; [[Quantization]]): NVFP4 proposed FP4 with a disentangled scaling/bit-width ablation; Jetson Thor now ships FP4 compute at scale. Validate NVFP4's disentangled-ablation claims on real FP4 hardware — a rare chance to check a compression paper's method against production silicon.
