# Weekly Digest — 2026-10-05

Five items, four of them squarely at the microcontroller tier. Two revisit assumptions that TinyML practice treats as settled — that INT8 is the cheapest usable precision on a Cortex-M, and that the memory-optimal in-place convolution formula is correct — and both find the received answer wrong on real hardware. A fabricated 28nm phase-change-memory in-memory-computing system-on-chip from a group unrelated to the earlier memristor and MTJ work gives the emerging-NVM hardware thread its third independent silicon anchor. An open-source RISC-V FPGA platform closes the gap between the TensorFlow Lite integer inference scheme and what open accelerator platforms actually execute, and a foundation-model deployment on a Qualcomm Hexagon NPU shows that *activation* memory and operator compatibility, not weight size, decide whether a model runs. Touches [[Quantization]], [[Cortex-M]], [[RISC-V]], [[NPU]], [[TensorFlow_Lite_Micro]], [[CMSIS-NN]], [[Vision]], [[Industrial_IoT]], [[Keyword_Spotting]], [[Federated_Learning]], [[On-device_Learning]], [[Event-Driven_Neuromorphic_Accelerators]] and [[MLPerf_Tiny]].

## 1. Right In-Place (RiP) Convolution: A Simple, General, and Near-Optimal Strategy for Memory-Efficient CNN Inference

**Source:** arXiv:2610.00586 (cs.LG; cross-listed cs.CV) — submitted 30 Sep 2026; extended version of a paper accepted at the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Global South in AI
**Authors:** Opegbemi Matthias Busoye, Tolulope Matthew Busoye, Eghonghon-aye Eigbe
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00586

**Why it matters:** On a [[Cortex-M]]-class part the binding constraint on CNN inference is peak activation memory, not MACs. The standard answer — in-place convolution over a shared workspace, as formalized by Gural and Murmann — has been treated as memory-optimal and correct. This paper shows the published closed form is wrong in two regimes and then gives a corrected, general and row-major-preserving replacement whose effect is measured on real microcontrollers with bit-identical outputs. It is a rare case of a foundational TinyML result being audited rather than built upon.

**Technical summary:** The authors identify (1) an under-allocation of exactly (k−1)·C_in mod (C_out−C_in) scalars in the Gural–Murmann bound, active on every convolutional layer of that work's own deployed network and manifesting as silent corruption of still-live input, and (2) an unbounded overestimate (up to 2,432×) once the critical leg leaves the output grid. They correct both and generalize to arbitrary stride, dilation, padding and rectangular kernels. Right In-Place (RiP) convolution reads each layer's input right-aligned in a shared workspace and writes its output left-aligned from index zero; the memory "debt" is piecewise affine in the output pixel index, so the minimum safe gap follows from O(1) breakpoint evaluation without enumerating the output grid, and the non-sequential traversal (which cost 2× inference time in transposes) is no longer needed. Across 10,000 random layers there was no corruption; across 84 layers from 25 architectures RiP matches the herringbone workspace exactly on 58 and within 5% on 81, using 24.8% less memory than dual buffering on average. Written into TinyEngine's kernels and deployed on a Raspberry Pi Pico 1 and Pico 2, it cuts peak activation memory across eleven MCUNet models by 12.5–33.3% at unchanged cycle counts, raising the number of MCUNet models that fit the Pico 1's 256 KB SRAM from six to nine.

**Novelty assessment:** Moderate-to-high. In-place convolution is known; the new contributions are the two documented failure regimes of the accepted bound, the generalization beyond valid-padding/unit-stride/odd-square kernels, and a closed-form safe-gap computation that keeps sequential access. Validation is on two real MCUs with cycle counts and bit-identical outputs; the abstract does not state whether the kernels are released, which should be checked before any deep-analysis record.

**Relevance score:** 4/5 — core-tier result with measured peak-memory reductions on real Cortex-M0+/M33 hardware and standard MCUNet models, directly relevant to [[TensorFlow_Lite_Micro]]/[[CMSIS-NN]]-style memory planners; workshop venue and unconfirmed artifact release keep it short of 5.

## 2. 16-bit Precision of Convolutional Neural Networks on Microcontroller Units for 8-bit Costs

**Source:** arXiv:2610.03402 (cs.LG) — submitted 2 Oct 2026
**Authors:** Rui Liu, Benjamin Paaßen
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03402

**Why it matters:** The TinyML default is W8A8 integer inference because 8-bit is assumed to be the fastest and most energy-efficient precision an MCU can run. This paper argues, from the Armv7E-M microarchitecture, that 16-bit weights and activations (W16A16) can be as fast and as energy-efficient as 8-bit on [[Cortex-M]] cores while cutting quantization error by roughly an order of magnitude. If it holds, the accuracy-versus-cost frontier that the [[Quantization]] concept page describes for microcontrollers is mis-drawn at the 8/16-bit boundary.

**Technical summary:** The work presents W16A16 as a high-precision, low-energy quantization scheme and analyzes the Armv7E-M architecture to explain why 16-bit arithmetic is competitive there. The abstract does not spell out the mechanism; a plausible reading, to be confirmed against the full text, is that the DSP extension's SIMD instructions operate on packed 16-bit halfwords, so 8-bit operands gain little beyond memory footprint once unpacking and requantization are counted. It evaluates empirical quantization error for regression and classification tasks and measures time and energy consumption in actual MCU deployment at both layer and model level, reporting ca. 10× lower quantization error than 8-bit schemes with similar or better inference time and energy.

**Novelty assessment:** Moderate. 16-bit fixed-point inference on Cortex-M is not new (CMSIS-NN has q15 kernels), but a controlled measurement of W16A16 against W8A8 on the same core, with an architectural explanation of the parity, is a useful and under-reported result. Open questions the abstract leaves: memory footprint (16-bit doubles weight storage, which is often the real limit on small parts), which 8-bit kernels served as baseline, and whether the parity survives on Armv8.1-M with Helium (MVE), which has genuine 8-bit lanes.

**Relevance score:** 4/5 — measured time and energy on a real Cortex-M, a result that directly bears on the [[Quantization]] and [[CMSIS-NN]] pages and on the measurement-realism thread (bit width does not predict runtime cost); preprint, single architecture, memory cost not reported in the abstract.

## 3. MEGATRON: a 28nm Analog PCM CiM/Digital System-on-Chip for Edge GenAI at 57.5 TOPS/W and 1.52 Mparam/mm²

**Source:** arXiv:2609.35254 (cs.AR) — submitted 28 Sep 2026
**Authors:** Alessandro Nadalini, Angelo Garofalo, Lorenzo Greco, Andrea Belano, Alessio Antolini, Francesco Zavalloni, Andrea Lico, Riccardo Zurla, Emanuela Calvetti, Luigi Croce, Marco Pasotti, Alessandro Cabrini, Eleonora Franchi Scarselli, Davide Rossi, Francesco Conti
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35254

**Why it matters:** A fabricated and measured heterogeneous SoC that pairs a non-volatile analog in-memory-computing engine (a 4 Mi-cell phase-change-memory array) with a digital [[RISC-V]]-based flexible [[NPU]], from an author group that (judging by the author list; affiliations are to be confirmed from the full text) spans the University of Bologna PULP line and STMicroelectronics' PCM team, independent of both the MTJ stochastic-computing program and the memristor depthwise-convolution SoC. Emerging-NVM in-memory computing is a known gap in the Hardware taxonomy; this is the third fabricated-silicon anchor from a third unrelated group, and the first to target generative edge workloads explicitly.

**Technical summary:** MEGATRON is a 28nm FD-SOI system-on-chip combining PCM-based analog compute-in-memory (PCiM) with RISC-V processors and a digital neural processing unit. The abstract reports up to 3.5 TOPS/W on the RISC-V cluster and 57.5 TOPS/W on the PCiM engine, at a storage density of 1.52 Mparam/mm² with 4-bit effective weight precision. The design targets "Edge GenAI", i.e. small generative/transformer-class models rather than the classification CNNs that most TinyML silicon reports on.

**Novelty assessment:** Moderate-to-high. PCM-based analog CiM has been demonstrated at larger scale (the IBM 64-core chip), but integrating a PCM CiM engine with a programmable RISC-V/NPU subsystem in a single edge SoC, with a density figure in parameters per mm², is a systems contribution. The arXiv abstract is a two-sentence summary: workload, accuracy under analog non-idealities, and end-to-end (not engine-only) efficiency need the full text before any record is created.

**Relevance score:** 4/5 — measured silicon at the edge tier, a third independent anchor for an open Hardware-branch gap, and a direct link between in-memory computing and [[Generative_EdgeAI]]; held back by an abstract that gives no benchmark, no accuracy and no end-to-end numbers.

## 4. ZTA-Q: an Open-source RISC-V Platform for Accurate Quantized CNN Inference

**Source:** arXiv:2610.01867 (cs.AR) — submitted 1 Oct 2026; accepted at IEEE ICECS 2026
**Authors:** Yike Li, Ajay Kumar M, Vishnu PS, Dimitrios S. Nikolopoulos, Bo Ji, Hans Vandierendonck, Deepu John
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01867

**Why it matters:** The TensorFlow Lite integer inference scheme (per-channel symmetric weights, asymmetric activations, fixed-point requantization multipliers and shifts) is the de-facto interchange format for deployed [[Quantization]], yet most open-source accelerator platforms implement only an approximation of it, so a model that is accurate in the reference interpreter can silently lose accuracy on the hardware. ZTA-Q is an open [[RISC-V]] platform built to execute that scheme faithfully, and — more useful for the Observatory — it exposes the requantization datapath as a configurable study instrument for how circuit-level shortcuts affect model accuracy.

**Technical summary:** ZTA-Q extends an open RISC-V accelerator platform with the operator support needed for end-to-end TensorFlow Lite INT8 CNNs and adds a configurable post-processing datapath in which reduced multiplier precision, shared shift scaling and simplified rounding can be switched on independently. Implemented on a Digilent Arty A7-100T FPGA at 83.3 MHz, the extensions cost 26.3% LUT, 12.6% register and 150% DSP overhead and keep top-1 and top-5 degradation on representative CNNs within 0.25 percentage points.

**Novelty assessment:** Moderate. Open RISC-V CNN accelerators exist; the specific contribution is fidelity to the TFLite integer scheme and the controlled-approximation datapath, which turns a deployment platform into a measurement harness for requantization effects. FPGA-only (no ASIC numbers), and "representative CNN models" are not named in the abstract. A companion ICECS paper from the same group, AdaptViT (see "Also noted"), applies a runtime-adaptive single-binary pruning scheme to a custom RISC-V core.

**Relevance score:** 4/5 — open-source hardware at the Observatory's core tier, a reusable harness for the toolchain-fidelity questions raised by the INT8-portability work, and a clean link between [[TensorFlow_Lite_Micro]]'s numerics and [[RISC-V]] silicon; FPGA-only and venue-limited.

## 5. DCM-SAM: Defect-Conditioned Mixture of LoRA Experts for NPU-Deployed AM Defect Segmentation

**Source:** arXiv:2609.38811 (cs.CV; cross-listed cs.LG) — submitted 30 Sep 2026; accepted at the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on On-Device Intelligence (ODI)
**Authors:** Md Mushfiqur Rahaman, Md Mahedi Hasan, Imtiaz Ahmed, Srinjoy Das
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38811 — code link in the abstract

**Why it matters:** An [[Industrial_IoT]] inspection task (pores and inclusions in X-ray CT slices of metal additive-manufacturing parts) that must run at the machine, and a deployment section that measures what adaptation papers rarely do: whether the adapted foundation model actually allocates and runs on the target [[NPU]]. The findings — ViT-H and ViT-L compile but cannot allocate at 1024×1024 because activations, not weights, exceed the device ceiling; weight quantization does not help; the adapted ViT-B fails where the stock one succeeds until a numerically identical attention rewrite — are the same "deployability is a toolchain/operator property" fact seen on microcontroller NPUs, now documented on a Qualcomm Hexagon at the smartphone/industrial-gateway tier.

**Technical summary:** One frozen Segment Anything backbone carries a separate Conv-LoRA expert bank and mask decoder per defect class, each trained in its own pass without prompts on synthetic slices alone, updating 4.4% of parameters. On the XCT-SAM benchmarks, DCM-SAM on a ViT-B backbone beats every baseline for both classes against XCT-SAM's ViT-H, and reaches 64.2% pore IoU on real NIST scans with no real images seen in training. After the attention rewrite, the complete DCM-SAM runs on the Hexagon NPU in FP16 at 1024×1024 with no operator falling back to the CPU and masks within 0.01% of pixels of the FP32 reference.

**Novelty assessment:** Moderate. Class-conditioned LoRA experts over a frozen SAM is an incremental adaptation design; the value is the synthetic-only training transferring to real scans and, above all, the explicit on-NPU allocation study showing activation memory and graph structure as the binding constraints. Workshop paper; no latency or energy figures in the abstract.

**Relevance score:** 3/5 — relevant and credible, with real-NPU deployment evidence that strengthens the operator-set/toolchain thread from a second accelerator family, but on a tier above the Observatory's core and without measured latency or energy.

## Signals for the knowledge base

Three of this week's items say the same thing from different directions: on edge hardware, the cost and the feasibility of a model are decided by the runtime and the memory system, not by bit width or parameter count. W16A16 is reported as cheap as W8A8 on Armv7E-M for reasons the authors locate in the microarchitecture; the memory-optimal in-place bound was silently wrong on the very network it was deployed with; a foundation model that compiles for a Hexagon NPU cannot allocate because of activations, and weight quantization does not change that. Together with the restricted-operator-set observations on STM32N6 and Akida, this argues for treating "deployability and cost are runtime properties" as the organizing idea behind both the measurement-realism and the operator-set gaps, and for deciding them together. MEGATRON supplies the third independent fabricated anchor for emerging-NVM in-memory computing; the reviewer deciding that Hardware gap should read it alongside IMC-CLINIC, which addresses the ADC-quantization error such engines introduce.

## Suggested thesis / research hooks

- **Re-measure the 8-bit default across Cortex-M generations:** paper 2 attributes the W16A16/W8A8 parity to the Armv7E-M microarchitecture. A well-scoped Master's project would repeat the layer- and model-level time/energy measurement on Armv7E-M (M4/M7), Armv8-M without MVE (M33) and Armv8.1-M with Helium (M55/M85), using [[CMSIS-NN]] q7 and q15 kernels and the [[MLPerf_Tiny]] models, and report memory footprint alongside speed and energy — the quantity the abstract omits and the one that usually decides on sub-512 KB parts.
- **Audit the memory planners:** paper 1 found the accepted in-place bound under-allocates on real layers. [[TensorFlow_Lite_Micro]]'s offline memory planner, TinyEngine, microTVM's USMP and the ST Edge AI tool each make their own assumptions about buffer reuse. A reproducible audit that takes the eleven MCUNet models plus the MLPerf Tiny four through each planner, records peak activation memory and checks bit-identity of outputs against a reference, would both validate RiP independently and give the measurement-realism thread a memory-side benchmark.
- **A requantization-fidelity harness on open RISC-V hardware:** ZTA-Q's configurable post-processing datapath can switch multiplier precision, shared shifts and rounding independently. Sweeping those knobs across the MLPerf Tiny models and tabulating accuracy loss per shortcut would quantify how much of the "INT8 is not portable" effect is attributable to requantization arithmetic alone, separate from operator coverage.
- **Activation-ceiling-aware adaptation:** DCM-SAM shows the adapted encoder fails to allocate on the NPU where the stock one succeeds, until an attention rewrite. A thesis could formalize an "allocation budget" check (peak activation tensor size and operator graph shape under the vendor compiler) as a constraint inside LoRA/adapter design, and test it on Hexagon QNN, Ethos-U Vela and ST Neural-ART toolchains.
- **Edge GenAI on PCM in-memory compute:** MEGATRON reports 57.5 TOPS/W on the PCiM engine at 4-bit effective weights. Open questions that would make a strong PhD-level project with access to the full paper: which transformer-class workload was measured, how analog non-idealities and the ADC path (IMC-CLINIC's object of study) affect generation quality, and what the end-to-end — not engine-only — efficiency is once the RISC-V/NPU side handles attention and nonlinearities.
